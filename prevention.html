Breast cancer Prevention

Can breast cancer be prevented?

There is no guaranteed way to prevent breast cancer. However, there are ways to reduce the risk of developing it. Although some risk factors, such as age and inherited genetic changes, cannot be controlled, certain lifestyle choices may help lower the risk.
Maintaining a healthy lifestyle, including staying physically active, maintaining a healthy body weight, avoiding alcohol, and eating a balanced diet, may help reduce the risk of breast cancer. However, these measures cannot eliminate the risk entirely.

Early Detection

Although early detection does not prevent breast cancer from developing, it can help identify the disease at an earlier stage, when treatment may be more effective.
Recognizing unusual breast changes and seeking medical advice promptly are important steps. Depending on a person's age and individual risk, healthcare professionals may also recommend screening, such as mammography.

Understanding Your Risk

Some factors that influence breast cancer risk cannot be changed. These include increasing age, inherited genetic changes, and family history.
People with a strong family history of breast cancer may benefit from discussing their individual risk with a healthcare professional.

Why Prevention Matters

Reducing the risk of breast cancer and improving early detection can help lessen its impact on individuals and communities. However, access to health education, screening, and medical care varies around the world.
Improving awareness and making healthcare services more accessible are important steps toward reducing the global burden of breast cancer.

Remember

Reducing risk is not the same as eliminating risk. Breast cancer can develop even in people who maintain a healthy lifestyle and have no known risk factors.
