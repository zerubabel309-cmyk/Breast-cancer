Symptoms of breast cancer

Recognizing the Signs

Breast cancer can cause changes in the breast or surrounding areas. Recognizing possible symptoms and seeking medical advice promptly can help with early diagnosis. Some people with breast cancer may not notice any symptoms in the early stages.

Common Signs and Symptoms

A lump in the breast

A new lump or thickened area in the breast or underarm may be a sign of breast cancer. Many breast lumps are harmless, but new or unusual lumps should be checked by a healthcare professional.

Change in breast size or shape

A noticeable change in the size, shape, or appearance of one breast may require medical evaluation.

Changes in the skin

The skin may become dimpled, puckered, red, swollen, or resemble the texture of an orange peel.

Changes in the nipple

The nipple may turn inward, change appearance, or develop unusual skin changes.

Unusual fluids from nipple

Fluid coming from the nipple, especially if it contains blood or occurs without squeezing, should be assessed by a healthcare professional.

Persistent breast discomfort

Pain is not usually the only sign of breast cancer, but persistent or unusual discomfort should not be ignored.

Why you should ask medical advice?

Not every breast change is caused by cancer. Many changes result from harmless conditions. However, if you notice a new or persistent change, seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional rather than attempting to diagnose it yourself.
